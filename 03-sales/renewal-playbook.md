# Renewal Playbook — Meridian Research & Advisory

**Purpose:** Hit the renewal target (≥90%) with discipline, evidence, and early intervention. Renewal is not a one-month event — it's a 90-day managed process that starts at onboarding.

**Core belief:** a renewal is won by the value delivered in months 1–9, not by the pitch in month 10.

---

## 1. The 90-Day Renewal Cadence

Every subscription/retainer account runs this timeline. CRM flags every renewal at day 90 out.

| T-minus | Action | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90 | Flag in CRM; pull value evidence pack (usage, deliverables, feedback) | Halo |
| 90 | Baseline health check: usage, feedback, satisfaction pulse | Halo |
| 60 | Value recap call #1 (see Section 2); confirm scope still fits | Halo |
| 60 | At-risk assessment (Section 4); if flagged → save plays (Section 5) | Halo + head of sales |
| 45 | Renewal proposal drafted (re-scope, price, expansion options) | AE |
| 45 | Renewal proposal sent; buyer + budget holder in the room | AE |
| 30 | Decision call; handle objections (negotiation playbook); confirm auto-renewal | AE |
| 14 | Signed renewal OR formal save escalation | AE + head of sales |
| 0 | Renewal confirmed; auto-renewal verified; onboarding-to-continuation handoff | CS |

**Rule:** if no renewal decision by T-30, the head of sales is escalated automatically. Silent renewals do not happen by accident.

---

## 2. Value Recap (evidence, not fluff)

The value recap is the renewal's backbone. It uses the client's own usage and words.

**Value evidence pack — assemble by T-60:**
- [ ] Deliverables delivered vs. promised (list with dates)
- [ ] Usage stats (briefs read, scans requested, questions answered, product logins)
- [ ] Feedback quotes from check-ins (their words, verbatim)
- [ ] The success measures they named at kickoff (onboarding playbook) mapped to what we delivered
- [ ] Qualitative wins: decisions they made using our work, competitive saves, market moves they caught
- [ ] Quantified where honestly supportable (internal cost avoided, time saved) — no ROI guarantees, only measured/observed facts

**Value recap call #1 (T-60) structure (30 min):**
1. What we delivered (2–3 minutes, concrete list).
2. What it moved for them (their words, read back).
3. What's next for their business (growth/market changes).
4. The renew question: "Is this still the right shape, or should it change?"

**Golden rule:** if you cannot assemble an evidence pack (no usage, no deliverables), that IS the at-risk signal — fix value, don't just pitch renewal.

---

## 3. Re-scope & Price Adjustment

Renewals are re-scoped to reality, not copy-pasted.

| Change in their world | Re-scope action |
| --- | --- |
| Usage down (briefs unread) | Reduce scope or convert to lighter tier; do NOT just reprice up |
| Usage up + growth | Expand scope / add vertical (see upsell playbook); price up with value recap |
| Market/company change (funding, M&A, new exec) | Re-confirm decision maker; re-scope to new priorities |
| Stable + happy | Renew as-is; bundle an expansion option as option B |

**Price adjustment rules:**
- Price increases require a value story (deliverables added, usage growth). Never raise price on a flat account.
- Price increases are announced with 30–60 days notice (contract + good manners).
- Renewal price changes follow the negotiation ladder (≤10% deviation = standard; >10% = CEO).
- Grandfathering: minor increases can be deferred a cycle for high-health accounts to protect ≥90% renewal.

**Renewal proposal format:** same 8-section structure as the proposal playbook, with the value recap replacing the problem statement, and Option B = renewal + expansion.

---

## 4. At-Risk Signals & Health Scoring

Monitor these signals continuously; act at first flag, not at renewal season.

| Signal | Weight | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Usage drop >40% vs. trailing 3-month average | High | Value not landing |
| Missed check-ins / silence from client >30 days | High | Disengagement |
| Negative feedback (logged in CRM) | High | Trust damaged |
| Churn of our champion (their role changed/left) | High | No internal advocate |
| Budget pressure mentioned | Medium | Price issue |
| Scope disputes / late invoicing | Medium | Relationship strain |
| Deliverable slippage (ours) | High | Our failure → fix fast |
| No replies on 2 consecutive touches | High | Gone dark |

**Health score:** compute per account monthly (Halo). `Green` ≥ 8/10 → normal cadence. `Yellow` 5–7 → proactive call within a week. `Red` < 5 → immediate save play (Section 5) with head-of-sales visibility.

**Every account is scored monthly, logged in CRM, reviewed in the monthly win/loss/renewal review.**

---

## 5. Save Plays

For `Yellow`/`Red` accounts, run a save play before any renewal conversation.

| Play | When | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value rescue | Usage down / value unclear | Pull the evidence pack; if thin, do a free value-add (a quick market scan) to re-prove value fast; book a value re-anchor call |
| Advocate rebuild | Champion churned | Find the new decision maker; do a fresh kickoff-style meeting (onboarding playbook, abbreviated); rebuild the evidence trail with the new owner |
| Scope fix | Deliverables not landing | Re-scope to what they actually use; offer a reduced-tier renewal to keep the door open — cheaper to keep them small than lose them |
| Price relief | Budget pressure | Down-tier the plan (smaller scope, lower price) — protect renewal %, accept lower MRR temporarily; never give a free month as a bribe |
| Trust repair | Our slippage/complaint | Deliverable guarantee plan: named owner, dates, weekly updates until trust returns; escalate internally |

**Save-play rule:** the goal is retaining a valuable account, not preserving revenue on a dead one. If a client is delivering zero value and wants out, let them exit cleanly (better than a cancelled-and-referred-for). Log the save outcome in CRM.

**Save escalation:** Red accounts get head-of-sales review within 48h. CEO is informed for any account >$5k/mo in Red state.

---

## 6. Auto-Renewal Configuration

Auto-renewal is the default mechanism for subscriptions; it protects the ≥90% target and reduces decision friction.

- [ ] Contract states auto-renewal terms (term, notice window, price adjustment notice).
- [ ] Billing system set to auto-renew; invoice issued on the renewal date.
- [ ] Client notified of renewal and price 30–60 days before (per Section 3) — auto-renewal ≠ silent renewal.
- [ ] Opt-out is easy and honored (a client who wants out gets out; churn a happy-clean exit beats a resentful account).
- [ ] CRM renewal task remains active even with auto-renewal — the commercial re-scope (Section 3) still happens.
- [ ] Any auto-renewal cancellation must go through the save play (Section 5) before it's final — one attempt to save, then respect it.

**Rule:** auto-renewal is about frictionless retention, not trapping clients. If a client says "we didn't know" — refund or adjust. Reputation beats a single invoice.

---

## 7. Renewal Metrics & Review

| Metric | Target | Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renewal rate | ≥90% | Monthly |
| MRR retention (gross) | ≥95% | Monthly |
| MRR retention (net, incl. expansions) | ≥105% | Monthly |
| At-risk accounts (% of book) | <10% | Monthly |
| Renewal cycle time (from proposal to sign) | ≤14 days | Monthly |
| Price changes (up/down) | tracked | Quarterly |

**Monthly renewal review (part of win/loss review):** every account in the renewal window for the next 90 days is reviewed — health score, evidence pack status, save-play status, expansion attached to renewal (upsell playbook). Output: a list of actions and owners.

**Feedback loop:** renewal loss reasons (price, value, moved internal, vendor switch, no decision) are logged verbatim into the win/loss data — they directly inform positioning and lead scoring.
