# Proposal Playbook — Meridian Research & Advisory

**Purpose:** Convert SQLs → proposals → signatures with structure, evidence, and disciplined bundling. Proposals must match the brand: clear, evidence-first, no hype, no guaranteed-ROI language.

**Gate:** No proposal leaves Meridian without passing the QA gate.

---

## 1. Proposal Structure (template)

Every proposal follows 8 sections, in order. Reuse the canonical template in `/templates/proposal-sow-template.md` — never free-form.

| # | Section | Content rules | Target length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Executive summary | 3–5 sentences: their situation, the decision on the table, what we propose, expected outcome framed as evidence of method, not guarantee | 0.5 page |
| 2 | Problem statement | Restate their problem in their words (from discovery). Quote their language. Neutral, no embellishment | 0.5 page |
| 3 | Solution | What Meridian does (services), how it maps to their problem, why evidence-first matters for them | 1 page |
| 4 | Deliverables | Concrete artifacts: report, deck, dataset, dashboard, workshop session. Named, countable, dated | 1 page |
| 5 | Timeline | Phases with dates and milestones; explicit start date and dependencies (access, data, stakeholders) | 0.5 page |
| 6 | Pricing | Options table (see bundling below). Total clearly stated. No "from $" ambiguity | 1 page |
| 7 | Terms | Payment schedule, cancellation, IP, confidentiality, your responsibilities vs. theirs | 0.5 page |
| 8 | Next steps | Signing steps, kickoff expectations, single owner, validity window (30 days) | 0.25 page |

**Validity window:** 30 days. State it. This creates scarcity without hype and forces the follow-up cadence (Section 5).

---

## 2. Discovery → Proposal Inputs

Do not write a proposal without these inputs from discovery:

- [ ] Confirmed problem statement in the prospect's words (verbatim quote captured).
- [ ] Decision maker(s) identified and in the room.
- [ ] Budget range or signal (asked directly).
- [ ] Timeline driver ("we decide by end of quarter").
- [ ] What "done" looks like to them (their success criteria).
- [ ] One verified evidence point (their industry, market, or competitor data) included in Section 3.
- [ ] Buying process: who signs, who pays, any procurement/legal review.

If any input is missing → do NOT send. Go back to discovery. A proposal written on assumptions converts at a fraction of one written on evidence.

---

## 3. Bundling Rules (attach subscription)

The **primary commercial objective is MRR attach ≥60%.** Structure options to make subscription the natural middle choice.

| Option | Structure | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Project only | e.g., AI Strategy Sprint at list price | Anchor; keeps project buyers |
| B — Project + subscription | Sprint + 3-month Intelligence Subscription, bundled discount 10–15% on the subscription | Recommended option — mark with a box/star |
| C — Engagement | Full retainer or research retainers + subscription, bundled | For accounts with clear ongoing need |

**Rules:**
1. Always present 3 options; never 1. Buyers need contrast.
2. Mark Option B as "Recommended" with a one-line reason tied to THEIR continuity need (e.g., "markets move quarterly — keep the evidence current").
3. Bundled discount must be visible as a line item (transparent, not hidden).
4. Never force subscription onto a project-only buyer; present it, don't trap it.
5. Renewal is pre-wired: Option B converts to a subscription renewal with project as add-on, not the reverse.

## 4. Objection-Proofing (before send)

Address known objections INSIDE the proposal so the objection is answered before it's asked:

| Likely objection | Pre-empt in |
| --- | --- |
| "We can do this internally" | Section 3: compare to internal build (opportunity cost, focus, independence) |
| "It's too expensive" | Section 6: show 3 options, monthly-equivalent cost, value framing per deliverable |
| "AI is hype" | Section 2/3: neutral framing, evidence-first method, no guarantees |
| "We don't have budget now" | Section 6: option B monthly structure; refer to payment terms |
| "Who is this company?" | Section 1: one line on Meridian OS + autonomy + human ownership (sole shareholder = accountable) |
| "What do we get exactly?" | Section 4: named deliverables, no vague "insights" |

Also include a short **Risks & assumptions** subsection in Terms: data availability, stakeholder access, timeline dependencies. Transparency here builds trust and reduces renegotiation.

---

## 5. QA Gate (mandatory before send)

Every proposal passes this checklist. No exceptions. Owner runs it; a second reviewer signs off.

- [ ] All 8 sections present, in order.
- [ ] No guaranteed-ROI claims; no "guaranteed", "best-in-class" hype words.
- [ ] Physical address + opt-out present in email (CAN-SPAM), if emailed.
- [ ] Pricing totals correct; math verified (run the numbers twice).
- [ ] Option B is recommended; subscription attach logic present.
- [ ] Prospect's verbatim problem quote included.
- [ ] Deliverables are countable and dated.
- [ ] Validity window stated (30 days).
- [ ] Names/spellings of all recipients correct.
- [ ] Timeline is realistic for current capacity.
- [ ] Terms match the standard template (no invented clauses).
- [ ] Second reviewer initialed the QA log.

**QA failure → fix → re-run.** Never send with known defects to "save time."

---

## 6. Follow-Up Schedule After Sending

Send, then follow the cadence. Do not go silent.

| Day | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Send proposal + short cover email (3 lines: what it is, the one ask, the link). Log in CRM. |
| +2 | If no open/no reply: brief nudge ("any questions on the plan or pricing?") |
| +4 | Value re-anchor: 1-line recap of the core outcome + the evidence point from Section 3 |
| +7 | Ask for decision; reference the 30-day validity window |
| +10 | Escalate: offer to walk through Section 6/7 with their finance or legal |
| +14 | Final: "closing the loop" message; if no response, mark proposal `Lost` (reason: no decision) or pause with re-engage date |

**Internal rule:** a proposal older than 14 days with no movement is flagged in CRM for a weekly pipeline review. Revisions requested → re-run the QA gate on the revised version before re-sending.

---

## 7. Closing & Hand-off

- Signed proposal → immediately trigger onboarding (see `onboarding-playbook.md`).
- Terms confirmed (PO, invoice address, start date) before kickoff.
- Update CRM: won, amount, product lines, MRR attach flag, source, cycle length.
- Send a one-line confirmation of next steps + calendar invite for kickoff within 48 hours.

**Metrics you must log:** time-to-proposal, proposal→sign time, options chosen (A/B/C), MRR attach yes/no. These feed the win/loss and upsell playbooks.
