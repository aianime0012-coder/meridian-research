# Negotiation Playbook — Meridian Research & Advisory

**Purpose:** Negotiate to win at target economics without undermining the brand's evidence-first positioning. Discipline over discounting; structure over price cuts.

**Compliance/brand note:** never concede by adding ROI guarantees or hype to close. Concessions are structural (scope, timing, payment), not reputational.

---

## 1. Authorization & Concession Ladder

All concessions are pre-approved at the levels below. Anything outside the ladder requires CEO sign-off.

| Tier | Authority | Concession allowed | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | AEs/CS | Freebie add-on (e.g., one extra hour of training, one extra brief) | ≤ $500 value |
| 2 | AE lead / deal desk | Discount on any single line | ≤ 5% of deal |
| 3 | AE lead + head of sales | Discount or bundle adjustment | ≤ 10% of deal |
| 4 | CEO | Discounts, scope redefinition, unusual payment terms | **>10% deviation → CEO only** |

**Rule:** any deal price deviation >10% from list requires CEO approval in writing (email or CRM note). This is not skippable and it is not "rubber-stamped" — the CEO reviews margin and MRR attach before signing.

**Non-financial concessions (no approval needed, use freely):**
- Faster start date (capacity permitting)
- Choice of report delivery format
- Additional stakeholder review slot
- Quarterly (vs. monthly) invoicing
- Naming/attribution rights on a case study

---

## 2. Anchoring

- **Anchor first, never let the buyer anchor first.** State your price in the proposal and hold it in conversation. Meridian's anchor is the Option B (recommended) total.
- Anchor with **value, then price**: deliverable value → evidence → then number. Never quote price without the value frame in the same sentence.
- Anchor with **monthly-equivalent** for subscriptions (e.g., "$2.5k/mo to stay current on a market that shifts quarterly"), not just the annual total.
- If the buyer anchors first with a low number: do not accept, do not counter their number — reset to value ("if I can show you the market moves in your category, the decision isn't really about the number — here's what we'd scope for that").

**Reference points you can use (evidence, not hype):**
- Cost of an internal hire to do this work (their job req, their comp range).
- Cost of a bad AI decision (their current spend on vendor evaluations / failed pilots, if disclosed in discovery).
- Cost of a single wrong competitive read on a quarter's roadmap.

## 3. Common Objections & Responses

| Objection | Response framework | Script anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "It's too expensive" | Reframe to monthly-equivalent + what's inside + compare to internal cost | "Compare it to one month of a senior hire. You'd get more continuity, at a fraction of the cost, with no hiring risk." |
| "We'll do it internally" | Acknowledge capability, separate it from capacity + independence | "You absolutely can. The question is focus — your team's priority is shipping, ours is evidence. And an outside view is often what you're actually paying for." |
| "Not now / later" | Find the real blocker (budget, timing, authority); compress scope, don't discount | "Is it the timing or the budget? If budget, we can start with the Sprint and hold the subscription." |
| "Why an AI company for strategy?" | Prove method, not authority; name the autonomy/ownership structure plainly | "Because the tools are changing weekly — we run Meridian OS agents that read markets continuously, and a human owner signs every deliverable. You get machine scale with human accountability." |
| "We need to think about it" | Ask what they need to decide; offer a decision aid (walkthrough, references, sample deliverable) | "What would resolve it fastest — a walkthrough of a sample report, or a reference call?" |
| "Can you match [competitor]?" | Never price-match blindly; compare scope, not number | "I'd need to see their SOW. If it's the same deliverables, we can talk — but we usually find the scope differs, and we don't want to sell you something we won't deliver." |
| "Discount or we walk" | Use the ladder; if below floor, walk away gracefully | "That's below what we can do and keep the quality you need. I'd rather not take it than deliver something that fails you." |

**Rule of three:** maximum three concession rounds per deal. After that, either the deal closes at the ladder floor or it goes to `Lost`. Rounds = negotiation fatigue + margin death.

## 4. Deal Structure Options

Use structure to solve objections instead of cutting price:

| Situation | Structure lever |
| --- | --- |
| Cash tight | Pay in 2 installments (50/50 at milestones) — costs you nothing, removes blocker |
| Budget approval hard | Start with a smaller entry (Sprint) + written expansion path (subscription after 90 days) |
| Procurement wants annual | Annual subscription at 2 months free equivalent, locked — improves MRR retention |
| Internal justification needed | Quarterly invoicing, PDF-ready summary they can forward to finance |
| Scope creep risk | Cap hours/deliverables clearly; offer pre-paid add-on blocks (e.g., 5 extra report credits) |
| "We want a pilot" | Paid pilot = Sprint at low-end of band, not free. Never do free pilots |

**Hard rule:** no free work. No "pilot" without a price. A discounted pilot is fine; a free pilot trains the buyer that Meridian's value is negotiable to zero.

---

## 5. MRR Attach During Negotiation

Negotiation is where MRR attach ≥60% is won or lost. Guard it:

- When a buyer negotiates the project price down, **hold the subscription line** — restructure the project, not the subscription.
- When they ask "what can you drop to lower price?" — the answer to drop is the project scope or timeline, never the subscription attach in a bundled option.
- When they push to "start with just the project," agree but name the follow-up: "Great — and if the report's useful, the subscription is the natural next step. Let's schedule that conversation at delivery."

Log the attach decision in the CRM deal record: `Subscription attached: yes/no`, and the reason for no (feeds win/loss and upsell).

---

## 6. Walk-Away Rules

Walk away — gracefully, with the door open — when:

- Price demands go below the CEO-authorized floor (>10% deviation unapproved).
- Buyer demands scope that makes delivery quality impossible (report you can't stand behind).
- Buyer asks for guaranteed results/ROI (compliance + brand non-negotiable).
- Buyer's timeline requires delivery you can't do well.
- Buyer signals they'll never buy ("pricing fishing") — three rounds used.
- Buyer demands free work or a free pilot.

**Walk-away script:**

```
I appreciate the push. We can't hit that number and still deliver
the quality you need, and we'd rather not start a relationship
that disappoints you. If the budget changes next quarter, the door
is open — and here's a partial brief you can use internally.

```

Log walk-aways in CRM with the reason (price floor, scope, compliance, quality). These are lost-for-now, not lost-forever — set a re-engage date.

---

## 7. After Close

- Confirm final terms in writing (price, scope, structure, attach).
- Update CRM: final amount vs. list, concession tier used, attach flag, cycle length.
- If concession tier 3+ was used, one-line note on why (feeds pricing intelligence).
- Hand to onboarding within 48 hours (see `onboarding-playbook.md`).
